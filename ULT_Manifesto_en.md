## 1.Unity
### 1.1.Basic Background
#### 1.1.1.Profit-seeking and Harm-avoiding Instinct
Terrans(human beings) are a kind of animal. They began to stand out from other organisms about 50 million years ago and gradually developed into the ruler of the earth.  
50 million years is not too long in the long long evolution of organisms.  
Therefore, humans share quite a few Commonalities with the vast majority of animals that have not yet been eliminated by natural selection.  
The most prominent of these is the profit-seeking and harm-avoiding instinct; animals lacking this instinct have almost all become extinct.

At birth, human nature is inherently evil.  
Many people believe that `at birth, human nature is inherently good` and that humans possess a `humanity` that other animals lack, drawing a clear distinction between themselves and the savagery of other animals.  
However, due to animal instincts, humans are naturally endowed with an `evil` nature since they were born.  
This `evil` is not purely a derogatory term, but rather `for profit-seeking and harm-avoiding, ignoring the interests of others`.

But why do most people behave in a way that reflects their `humanity` and clearly distinguishes them from beasts?  
I do not deny the existence of humanity; I just believe it is not an instinct but something acquired through growing up in human society and learning by example.  
Very strong evidences are [feral child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feral_child) and [Chantek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chantek).  
Although feral children are human, they were raised by other animals from a young age and have adopted the habits of those animals entirely, making it hard to summarize the word `humanity` from them.  
Although the orangutan Shatke is not human, Shatke was raised as a human, so it is easy to observe the word `humanity` in him. However, because it was less intelligent than others, it ultimately never learned to speak, and eventually had its human rights stripped away due to violent behavior that revealed its inhuman savagery. The outcome was distressing, but for most of that long period, it displayed humanity; it showed its savagery only once.There are multiple reasons for this negative outcome. First, because it is not human, people hold it to higher standards, and it is inevitable that it will face discrimination and feel out of place in human society. Consequently, it can successfully integrate countless times, but as long as it makes a mistake once, almost everyone will speculate on it with the worst imagination. Second, the female classmate’s allegations of violence against it are unsubstantiated. I am not saying that Shateke must have been framed, but any human who wants can make baseless accusations against it, and such accusations are easily accepted, because people are naturally more inclined to believe their own kind than to believe Shatke. Third, it is not human after all, and it is unlikely to give it a fully human identity in human society. It will inevitably fall into the psychological struggle of identity and it is difficult not to be depressed.

Since `at birth, human nature is inherently good`, why is most of human society now showing the brilliance of humanity?  
I believe that tens of millions of years ago, human society must have been little different from animal herds, and the same kind of people hunted each other savagely for various interests.  
But the wisdom derived from the human brain is ultimately different from beasts'. After a long period of bloodshed, some ancient sages—who had gained greater mastery over their own wisdom—realized the destructive power of mutual slaughter and that cooperation brought greater benefits than plunder. Thus, they began to extend cooperation that existed only between spouses and blood relatives to a larger human race, from which the rudiments of `humanity` emerged, and then this extra-instinctive characteristic is passed down from generation to generation by memes rather than genes through word of mouth.  
Gradually expanding from the family to the wider community, family -> tribe -> country.

`humanity` can make people reluctant to infringe on the interests of others to a certain extent.  
However, `reluctant to infringe on the interests of others` do have a range decay, the closer one is to oneself, the less willing one is to infringe upon his interests. From close to far: spouse and blood relatives -> neighbourhood -> same hometown and customs -> similar dialects -> same ethnic group -> similar appearance characteristics -> same country -> same language -> foreigner  
Therefore, the most perfect humanity has not spread to the whole human society. Many different countries, forces, parties, groups and people do not hesitate to infringe on the interests of others to obtain their own interests.  
It is difficult for acquired habits to completely suppress innate instincts.
#### 1.1.2.The Contingency of History
Many political science theories with widespread influence, such as the [End of History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_of_history) and Marxism, hold that the trend of history is inevitable; the debate is just what the end of history should be.  
But I think history is inherently driven by contingency.  
Such as the [Soviet nuclear submarine in the Cuban missile crisis](#a1soviet-nuclear-submarine-in-the-cuban-missile-crisis) incident, a lot of historical trends have been driven by the chance occurrence of rare events such as a few people overturning the majority.  
A more convincing example is that the emergence of human beings from animals is itself a small probability event of evolution, and it is obviously unrealistic to expect animal society to go to the inevitable end of those theories; the fact that Earth became a place capable of sustaining life is an even small probability event.

Of course, I do not deny that in specific historical periods, there may be many high probability regularities, but most of those regularities are effective only during stable development stages. When the development of these regularities approaches its limits, society becomes unstable, giving rise to a multitude of low probability paths—each of which can drive history toward a radically different course. **It is precisely these low probability events that drive history to leap into different periods, thereby manifesting different high probability regularities**. Transitional periods of change are often shorter than periods of stable historical development, which is why it is all too easy to fall into the illusion of inevitable trend.
#### 1.1.3.Strong Countries Bully Weak Countries
From ancient times to the present, strong countries have bullied weak countries, annexing their territory or plundering their interests.

There is an old saying in China that summarizes the historical regularity of a certain  period: *The general trend of the world is that after a long period of division, there will inevitably be unification, and after a long period of c, there will inevitably be division.*  
It means that, given enough time, China’s various historical dynasties would inevitably cycle between periods of unified imperial rule and eras of fragmentation among competing warlords.  
After a long period of division, there will inevitably be unification. This fully illustrates the principle that strong countries bully weak countries.  
After a long period of unification, there will inevitably be division. This implies that no dynasty throughout history has escaped the cycle of history and has ultimately fragmented: This is why it is necessary to do `stability maintenance` after `unity`.  
The reason why the unification is limited to China is that ancient science and technology are not developed enough, and the lack of administrative ability and communication ability makes expansion limited. For example, the Mongolian Empire and Alexander Empire eventually split up, since they took too long for their decrees to spread from the capital to the frontier.

The world outside China is also full of wars between countries, and finally there are several watersheds between war and peace: World War I, World War II, nuclear deterrence system.  
Before World War I, the rulers of various countries considered whether to wage war based solely on `how much benefit could be gained`, `whether they could win` and `how much the war would cost`.  
World War I caused many European monarchs to lose their titles and imposed heavy costs on numerous countries; consequently, the League of Nations was established.  
This led the rulers of various nations to begin, for the first time, to question the legitimacy of war itself, regardless of its causes. Previously, waging war in response to the assassination of a crown prince had always been considered a completely justifiable cause; however, the unprecedented devastation of World War I finally prompted people to reflect on the war itself, rather than merely whether it had a justifiable cause.  
The whole world was relatively peaceful in the following decades. However, due to unavoidable reasons such as `instinctive hunger for interests`,` the victorious countries of World War I bullied Germany in a non-war manner `and` appeasement policies towards the countries that launched the war `, the international order established by World War I was broken.  
World War II broke out, and it was far more devastating than World War I, which led to the emergence of a new international order—the United Nations.  
Leaders around the world are once again learning the lessons of the past—just as they did immediately after World War I—and are carefully reconsidering the peace.  
But even so, the Cold War continued without starting wars, using every means other than war.  
Later, the five permanent members of the UN Security Council and a few other countries acquired nuclear weapons, and there are also unpredictable [dead-hand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Hand) like systems, forming a nuclear deterrent system.  
war against nuclear states and their common defense alliances is often hard or confined within a certain range.  
But the war against non-nuclear states has not yet been curtailed, however that's not the point.

The point is that the peace brought about by the watershed is temporary and fundamentally unstable.  
And so, over time, reflecting on the rationality of war itself regardless of its causes, is forgotten by the rulers of nations.  
In recent years, Russia's aggression against Ukraine, a country protected by [Budapest Memorandum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budapest_Memorandum) of many nuclear-armed countries, is a proof of this.  
Promises of protection by the countries concerned have become empty talk, just like the policy of appeasement before the start of World War II.
#### 1.1.4.Science and Technology Advancements Enhance War Intensity
This is very obvious, but since it is indeed a basic premise, I wrote it down.  
The more advanced science and technology, the more powerful the lethality of weapons, from wooden weapons, stone weapons, bronze weapons, iron weapons to hot weapons, and then from early guns to bombers, and now missiles and nuclear weapons.  
The greater the lethality of weapons (of course, not just weapons, but also economic impacts), the greater the scale of war will be.  
Many times people will joke that ancient warfare is just a village fight, which is caused by the low killing efficiency of ancient weapons.  
Imagine millions of warriors fighting each other with wooden weapons. That's nonsense.  
The more lethal the weapon, the more combatants it takes to sustain the wear and tear, the greater the casualties, and the greater the proportion of relatives and friends of the casualties among the population, sharply increasing the hatred between the warring parties, including non-combatants.  
Village fights are very easy to reconcile, but the hatred brought about by high-intensity wars like the world wars is difficult to resolve for many generations, and it is easy to trigger secondary liquidation wars through hatred.  
When the lethality of weapons reaches a certain threshold, the intensity of war will be great enough to destroy the entire human society.
## Appendix A: examples
### A.1.Soviet Nuclear Submarine in the Cuban Missile Crisis
During the Cuban Missile Crisis in the Cold War, the United States and the Soviet Union began a high-risk military confrontation.  
During that time, a Soviet nuclear submarine was surrounded by a U.S. fleet and forced to surface.  
There are several very high risk points in the process as follows:
1. The United States used depth charges to drive away the nuclear submarine; at the time, the sole application of this kind of depth charges was to drive away and force to surface, but the Soviet side was unaware of this, and the crew on board the submarine believed it was an aggressive move by the U.S. fleet.
2. There was a rule on Soviet nuclear submarines which is `If the submarine comes under attack and cannot establish contact with Moscow, the captain, first officer and political commissar may authorize the launch of nuclear weapons by unanimous decision.`. However, while all three did believe at the time that the ship had come under attack, one of them went against the consensus and vetoed the proposal to launch a nuclear weapon.
3. In the end, the Soviet nuclear submarine, unable to fight back, was forced to surface and prepare to accept the capture of the United States; however, the United States ultimately did not capture it but let it go.
4. Although the three decision-makers aboard the nuclear submarine prevented a nuclear war that would have destroyed mankind, the more than 80 crew members who eventually returned to the Soviet Union were all discharged from the military, and the first officer ultimately chose to take his own life. demonstrating that the Soviet Union’s internal policy toward surrender and compromise was one of severe repression, and that decisions made by the leadership would result in collective punishment of ordinary crew members who had no decision-making authority. Under such circumstances, the decision-maker's decision will be involuntary in many cases, and even reasonable decisions may be overturned from the bottom up.

It can be seen that the result of no nuclear war breaking out in the end is the result of the multiplication of many small probabilities. Perhaps in most parallel universes in quantum mechanics, mankind has long been destroyed by nuclear war.
